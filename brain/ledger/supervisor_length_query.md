## Why we are asking

The dissertation body stands at **28,332 words** on `texcount`, excluding bibliography and
appendices, against the **20,000 cap**. A reduction pass has been planned in full, priced against
measured realisation rates, and partly executed. We have a plan that complies — but it rests
entirely on one reading of one criterion, and we would rather have that reading confirmed or
corrected now than discover it was wrong after the body has been rewritten around it.

**Two questions. The first is the one that matters.**

---

## Q1 — Does R102 require the implication to be stated once per research question, or once per finding?

**This is not a request for guidance. It is the single assumption our compliance plan depends on,
and we would like it confirmed or corrected before we act on it.**

R102 reads: *"Results state what the findings imply for the research question(s)."*

**Our reading is that it quantifies over questions, not over findings.** Research question 4, for
example, is supported by five subsections of results. On our reading the Results chapter must
state what those findings imply *for RQ4* — once — and the supporting measurements may sit in an
appendix, with the body naming each finding and pointing at it. On the stronger reading, each of
the five findings must have its implication stated in the body.

**We have tested our reading per research question rather than applying it wholesale, and it does
not hold everywhere.** For RQ3 it fails: that question names two limbs (weather, and cross-series
pooling), the two limbs measure differently, and neither is secondary to the other, so both stay
in the body. That refusal is why we think the test is a real one and not a rationalisation.

**The consequence, stated plainly:**

| | Landing | Against 20,000 |
|---|---|---|
| **If our reading is correct** | 18,832 – 19,582 | **complies, 418 – 1,168 of margin** |
| **If the stronger reading governs** | 20,395 – 20,749 | **breaches by 395 – 749** |

The second row already assumes we have cut three sections of surveyed literature from the review.
**There is no further editorial lever.** If the stronger reading governs, we cannot reach 20,000
without removing findings from the dissertation, and we would want to discuss with you which
findings the work drops and what it therefore stops claiming — that is a decision about scope, not
about length, and it is not one we want to take by ourselves.

**What has already been done to get to 28,332 from where it started:** every derivation, secondary
table, protocol detail and reconciliation working moved to appendices; the protected set re-derived
twice against criteria quoted rather than paraphrased; compression applied at the rate our own
measured precedent supports; results answering no research question identified and relocated; and
the duplication between R102's site and R103's removed. What is left is material a criterion
requires in the body.

## Q2 — Are the appendices read and assessed?

**Roughly 4,900 words have been relocated to Appendices A–E on the assumption that they are, and
we have never checked it.**

Every derivation, every reconciliation working and every rejected alternative now sits there, with
the body carrying the finding and a cross-reference. If appendices are excluded from the word
count **and** not read closely, then what we have been doing is moving evidence out of the
marker's view rather than out of the count — which would be a materially worse outcome than being
over length, and would mean the strategy needs replacing rather than extending.

## Context, not asks

**Why the length concentrates where it does.** Demoting material to an appendix saves, per word
moved, according to which criterion governs the passage. These are measured, not estimated:

| Criterion governing the passage | Realised saving |
|---|---|
| None | 46 – 66 % |
| R83/R84 — a *reason* is one sentence | 84 % |
| **R102 — a finding, its number and its consequence** | **6 – 30 %** |

**The length is not distributed evenly. It concentrates exactly where the criteria demand in-body
content**, which is Results, and Results is where the project's contribution sits. Q1 is the
question of what that demand actually amounts to.

**Three groups of criteria compound for a measurement-dense project.** R83/R84 require every
rejected alternative's reason in Chapter 3. R102 requires the findings and their implications in
Chapter 4. R103 requires the same results answered again in Chapter 5. We have taken the position
that R102's minimum is the finding and its number, with the consequence left to R103's answer, and
§5.1 currently restates Chapter 4's confidence-set sizes, origin-count reversal, standard errors
and node counts with the numbers because both criteria ask for them.

**We are not asking for an extension or for permission to exceed the limit.**
