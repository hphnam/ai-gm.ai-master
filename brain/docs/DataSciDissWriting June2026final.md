# Writing the abstract

 Needs to stand alone i.e., be complete in itself
 Allows the reader to gain a very brief but complete overview of your entire report from aims to conclusions
  Does not act as an introduction (common mistake!)
  Typically 300 words
  Highly succinct but need to flow well – links between sentences
  Most important section along with discussion

# Writing abstracts

Whilst you will use most of the elements from the previous slide, they need to be carefully tailored to your study.
This means you may need to rearrange the order and adjust the length to describe your study effectively.
Try to write one sentence for each feature, with 2 or more for important elements.

<!-- Slide number: 28 -->

![Picture 11](Picture11.jpg)

| Section | Length | Purpose/Suggestions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | 1-2 sentences | Introduce the topic and the scientific problem/gap in the literature – you’re telling your reader why your research matters in the big picture |
| Aim | 1 sentence | Now your reader knows the background, clearly state the scientific aim of your paper |
| Method (design/implementation) | 2-3 sentences | How was the study done? Include approach and key information (e.g. population/species, sample size) but keep it brief. The reader can find all the specific values and processes in your methods! |
| Results | 2-4 sentences | What did the study find? Only mention the key results including specific statistical details (e.g. statistics, p-values etc.) |
| Conclusion | 1-2 sentences | Bring the results back to the introduction. How did or could your results address the scientific problem/gap? What future directives/research would you suggest? |

# Example 1 Deep Learning
At present, optical character recognition in natural scenes is a significant research direction in computer vision. In addition, high-performance text detection and recognition systems also have important practical significance in many application scenarios including document analysis, smart cities, and industrial detection. This report studies the related text detection and text recognition in natural scenes, including training text detectors based on deep learning with different natural scene datasets and training text recognition models based on deep learning with artificially generated datasets. Then we validated the performance of models separately. The text detection algorithm studied in this report performed well on natural scene datasets (ICDAR2015), and it achieved a precision of 77.8%. We used three evaluation methods in this part: recall, precision, and H-mean. The H-mean indicator is Recall and Precision, and we used it as a comprehensive evaluation indicator. In addition, we studied the related models of scene text recognition. This section looks at the recognition of numbers, English letters, and English punctuations. Due to the small number of open-source English character recognition datasets, we used an artificially generated English text recognition pair model based on the English corpus Conduct training. Furthermore, our recognition model used the CNN + RNN + CTC architecture, validated by experiments on artificially generated datasets. Finally, we got an accuracy of 95% on the synthetic dataset.

Example 2 Health Service Flow
Patient flow refers to the movement of patients within a service after they enter it. Analyzing patient flow can help healthcare providers improve service processes, rationally arrange medical resources, and reduce delayed treatment due to inappropriate services. This project aims to analyze the flow of mental health patients, mainly to solve two problems. One issue is understanding the movement of patients between the acute care pathways and routine care, analyzing the distribution of discharged patients (including those discharged too early), and the impact of other factors on discharge. Another question is to analyze the proportion of patients who moved between care teams and whether other factors influence movement. In order to answer these two questions, patient flow is mainly drawn by visualization method of Sankey diagram, and logistic regression models are used to predict the probability of discharge and team change. In conclusion, the pattern of patient flow varies regionally and over time. The distribution of discharged patients is more influenced by locality and service. The predictive accuracy of logistic regression model for discharge can reach 0.9, which can well predict whether patients can be discharged. In addition, patients’ movement between care teams is related to many factors (for example, service, date type, consultation medium, etc.), and the prediction accuracy of the model is 0.6482.

# Integrating sources into your text

When reviewing other studies, they need to be integrated into your own text, rather than read as a series of disconnected voices of other researchers (patchwork writing).

When you refer to another writer, you should begin and end in your own voice, with the middle part consisting of paraphrase or summary of the source and the final part a commentary on the contribution of this writer.

Harvey (1998:3-5) outlines 3 basic principles for integrating sources in academic writing:
