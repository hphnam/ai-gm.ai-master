MSc Data Science and Artificial Intelligence Dissertation Writing and Marking
Guidelines 2025/26

This document gives information on the MSc Data Science dissertation (DS591), including its
preparation, submission and assessment.

Please read and follow these guidelines carefully and examine the marking scheme (Appendix B) to
understand what constitutes a good quality dissertation. Note that you are required to arrange and
attend your dissertation viva, and to prepare a poster and attend Poster Conference on 11th
September 2026.

A suggested dissertation structure, with commentary, is provided in Appendix A. Example
dissertations can be seen on the DS591 Moodle.

Length
The dissertation must not exceed 20,000 words. Note that this is an upper limit and that
competence in producing a succinct and coherent report is essential. Reports that are unstructured,
overly verbose and contain irrelevant content will be penalised rather than rewarded. You should
discuss the length and content of your report with your supervisor.

A 12pt font and standard margins should be used. See also `Format and Presentation’ in Appendix A,
below.

Project specification
The project specification that you prepared at the start of your project period should be included as
an appendix.

An explanation of any significant differences between the scope of the project as described in the
specification and the project as performed should be given in the Discussion section. Note that
differences between the original specification and the project that was performed will not be seen
as a disadvantage where an account is given of how the project evolved.

Appendix A: Suggested Report Structure

Below is a suggested structure for the report. Note, however, that the structure may vary
depending on the specific project type and aims. For example, a project that involves the design of
a system, and is therefore more oriented towards computing, should explain the construct of the
system and its components; while a project that is more data analytic should focus upon the data
sourcing, the inherent data structure, data exploration and visualisation, the model formulation and
model evaluation; in line with the phases shaping the data science pipeline.

You should discuss a suitable report structure for your specific project with your supervisor.

Title
Your report should begin with a `title’ that succinctly describes the subject matter. Use descriptive
words that you would associate strongly with the content of your report.

Abstract
The `abstract’ should summarize, in one paragraph, the major aspects of the dissertation. The
paragraph should have a logical structure, for example, the question(s) you investigated (or aims),
the design/analytical methods used, the major findings (in context of the investigative questions
(aims)) and conclusions:

Introduction
This chapter should motivate your work; it should provide a clear statement of the aims of the
project and prepare the reader for what is to follow. You should provide a contextual overview (with
references) of the problem that you are tackling, with aspects such as why the project is worthwhile
(e.g. what is the current knowledge gap) and how the project may be beneficial to others. You
should clearly state the aims of the project (these should have been informed from your project
proposal) and you should follow this up by explaining the research question(s) that are to be
investigated. The chapter should conclude with a brief chapter by chapter overview of the
content/structure of the report.

Background/Related Work
This chapter should provide an account of the background reading and research that you have
performed. It should be structured as a systematic review using a concept-centric structure. As your
dissertation is a Masters-level piece of work, your writing should be critical: highlighting any
limitations of existing approaches and detailing how your work differs and overcomes such
limitations.

Methods This chapter will contain the details of the methodology that you applied/developed in
your project. You may be using existing methods in a novel application area, or you may be
contributing new methodology. In the latter case, the Background/Related Work section (outlined

above) should have clearly elicited the gap that your novel method fills, and as motivated in your
Introduction.

This chapter should be sufficiently detailed, (for example, with pseudocode diagrams, flow diagrams
of processes, unambiguous model definitions (typically numbered)) so that the reader would be able
to replicate your work. You should not include any source code here (except for perhaps very small
portions of code that represent a solution to a particularly interesting or difficult problem)
pseudocode is better). Use figures, as appropriate, to support your textual communication of
methods and procedures used.

This chapter should, as appropriate, detail methods of data sourcing, data preparation (e.g. cleaning,
removing outliers, integrating data, engineering of features etc.), the models that you implemented
(including precise details of the software and libraries used, if appropriate), and how the models
were applied / fitted and validated.

You should also consider, and reflect upon, whether any noise or bias could have been introduced
into your approach. For example, if you used a third-party tool and your work is reliant upon that
tool then the tool’s accuracy/performance may have impacted. Alternatively, if you were provided
with data which has inherent limitations, then this may have impacted the findings. You should
ensure that your methods section flows logically. You should try to clearly separate out the different
components of the project and work that you have performed. For instance, if your project is more
data analytical in nature, then you may elect to have sections describing the data and data source
and the exploratory data analyses. This may then be followed by a theory section detailing the
notation used, the models that you investigated, the estimation framework and how you validated
the identified models. The structure of this chapter(s) should be discussed with your supervisor to
ensure that you cover everything clearly and in a logical format.

Results
This chapter will focus on your findings including those relating to the methods of evaluating your
work. You should include both graphical and tabular information, supported by textual summaries of
your findings. You need to bring out the key features of your results. Tables should be created and
not copied from computer output. Ensure that you apply suitable statistical methods (as detailed in
the methods section) to compare model performance and to contrast different approaches.

For example, you should begin by explaining the procedures that you followed and include as much
details as possible about what your experimental settings were, how many data items you were
dealing with, what properties you set for your model and how (e.g. hyper-parameter tuning), and
what evaluation measures you used and why. This chapter should then describe the results from
your evaluation’s experiments, and what they imply (i.e. what do the results reveal in terms of your
research question(s)?).

Discussion
In this chapter you will reflect on your findings and what your analyses have revealed: In particular:

• What do the results reveal in relation to your research question(s)?
• Is your approach valid, and how can you argue this?
• Are there inherent limitations, including potential biases, in your work?
• How may any underpinning assumptions have impacted your findings?

Conclusions
This will be your final chapter (references and appendices will follow this) in which you will revisit
the general aim of your research and each of your objectives, reflecting on whether you have
achieved your general aim, or not. You should discuss the project as a whole, if you did it again
would you do it differently? What did you have to learn to do the project, what did you learn from
doing it? How would you modify your methodology if you were to do the project again?

Referencing
You are free to use any referencing format at your discretion, (Harvard, Numbering). Regardless of
which format of referencing you choose, ensure that you apply this format consistently throughout.

Format and Presentation
Your work must be word processed. The preferred package for any dissertation that contains
mathematical formula is Latex (see useful tools).

It is important that your report has a clear and attractive layout. It should be printed on A4 white
paper, a 12pt font and standard margins should be used. Make sure that you use past tense, as your
report is an account of work that has been performed.

Chapters should have a heading, e.g. Chapter 1. Introduction. Each chapter must start on a new page
(but sections should not, unless necessary). Chapters should have subsections numbered x.1, x.2.,
etc. Subsections can have subsections x.1.1, x.1.2 etc. It is not advisable to have a subsection
structure deeper than this.

Use a large and bold font for the main chapter headings and the title (centred). For first level
subsection titles: use bold and a font size slightly larger than the text. For lower-level subsection
titles, use smaller font (and bold and/or italic).

General Stylistic Points
Do not use bold and underlining together. Use the same style and size font of size 12 font for the
actual text throughout each chapter. All of the text and headings should be justified to the left hand
margin, i.e. do not left indent the text of subsections any further than that of enclosing sections. Use
consistently formatted paragraphs. Justified on both left and right edges, rather than ragged right

edge, is best. You can also use first line indent if you wish (but make sure it is used consistently).
Check your spelling and cross references very carefully. Use page numbering.

Figures
Within a chapter, number figures Figure x.1, Figure x.2, Figure x.3 etc. where x is chapter number.
Figures are predominantly pictorial or diagrammatical. Each figure should have a caption, e.g. Figure
4.3 System Architecture. Be consistent with caption style and positioning throughout the report.

Tables
Within a chapter, number tables Table x.1, Table x.2, Table x.3 etc where x is chapter number (note:
figures and tables are numbered independently of each other, e.g. first figure in chapter 2 is Figure
2.1, first table in chapter 2 is Table 2.1.). Tables are predominantly tabular in form and contain
mainly textual and/or numerical data. Each Table should have a caption, e.g. Table 4.3 Results of
performance evaluation based upon an analysis of X using model Y. Be consistent with caption style
and positioning throughout the report.

Referring to Figures and Tables
 Any figures and tables used must be referred to in the text. Try to position each table or figure close
to where it is first referenced. Refer to Figures/Tables properly, e.g. "as shown in Figure 3.1" or "(see
Figure 3.1)". The same rule applies to referring to figures and tables as for references, i.e. the way
the figure or table is referenced should not make the sentence ungrammatical.

Final Points
Above all, remember that writing is a process that involves iteratively re-reading and redrafting as
necessary. Allocate plenty of time for the write-up. You should start planning your report and writing
any parts as early as you can. Giving your supervisor a draft before the final deadline gives you
chance to incorporate the supervisor’s suggestions into the report and complete the final version in
the remaining time. Once again: check your spellings and all cross references very carefully.

Word processing: useful Tools
You can write the report using any word processing software that you are comfortable with. It is
advisable; however, that LaTeX should be used over Microsoft Office or LibreOffice Writer, given its
consistent formatting. A number of LaTeX resources have been made available on the module

Appendix B: MSc Data Science Marking Guide

The dissertation is marked out of 100 as below:

0-19 Fail
Little, or no, evidence of any knowledge is demonstrated and/or of any effort being made.

20-39 Fail
Some evidence of minimal effort being made is demonstrated. There may be a little logic or
structure to the document and some glimpses of understanding, however, the methodologies used
may be obviously incorrect or contain several major omissions.

40-49 Fail There is a clear and discernible structure and logic to the document. There is some
evidence of understanding and appropriate use of techniques, and no blatantly obvious major
mistakes or major omissions. However, no MSc level techniques have been used (or, if used, there is
little evidence of understanding) and/or the research question has not been answered sufficiently
well.

50-59 Pass
This should be a coherent and structured account where a research question is approached using
appropriate computational and statistical techniques, including one or more at MSc level. The topic
should be clearly motivated with appropriate background material. The account should be largely
correct and accompanied by relevant figures, tables and a bibliography which are, in turn,
articulated to illustrate the argument that addresses the research question. The techniques must
have been applied and interpreted correctly and there should be some demonstration that the
concepts behind the techniques have been understood.

60-69 Good Pass
As for a Pass but a) with some use of techniques or understanding of issues beyond those covered in
the MSc modules studied by the student; and b) demonstrating good conceptual or mathematical
understanding. The account should flow logically and be largely complete (for example, following the
data science pipeline, including: data- collection/sourcing/integration etc., exploratory data analysis
leading to the formulation of appropriate models and hypotheses for testing, validating of results
(e.g. by defending the experimental setup and procedures followed; by assessing model fit and
predictive capacity). The conclusion should demonstrate that the methodology has addressed and
answered the research question. This should be done in a competent, convincing and well-reasoned
manner.

70-79 Distinction
As for a Good Pass. In addition, the student should demonstrate a conceptual understanding and
proficiency computationally or statistically of either: a) An appropriate substantial body of

normally demonstrate a high level of insight, understanding and clarity, and the research question

80-100 Outstanding Distinction
As for a Distinction. However, the writing in the report must be of publishable quality (i.e. clear,

other than making it more concise, in a reputable journal.
