| Project description | GM-AI is a digital general manager for hospitality venues. It reads every SOP, runs opening and closing checklists, monitors stock, drafts supplier purchase orders, and is on WhatsApp all shift to answer staff questions. We’ve built it as a role, not a tool. Currently live across 4 Lune Brew Co venues, with external operators onboarding next.  Right now GM-AI is reactive. It answers when asked. This project builds the proactive brain: an AI that learns each venue’s normal operating rhythm and prompts the team before problems happen. Friday lunch sales running 30% above forecast? Prompt the manager that next week’s keg order should go up. Three staff have asked about the fryer reset this month? Flag a missing SOP. The brain notices what’s happening and decides what’s worth surfacing.  Research question: how can an LLM-based agent, given access to a hospitality venue’s operational data and tools, learn that venue’s normal operating rhythm and intervene proactively when reality deviates from it.  The brain has three components:  1. Learn the rhythm. Model what’s normal for each venue from sales, stock movements, checklist completions, and chat-log volume. The interesting research is in multi-venue transfer learning: each venue is small on its own, but together they offer a richer signal.  2. Notice deviation. Detect when reality has shifted meaningfully from the learned rhythm. Standard anomaly and change-point detection territory, made harder by the noise of real venue data and small samples per venue.  3. Reason and surface. An LLM-based agent that takes the deviation signals, has live access to operational tools (Xero, Square, the knowledge base), and decides what’s worth raising with the manager, when, and in what tone. This is where most of the unsolved research lives: agent design, tool-use evaluation, and how you tell whether an agent’s judgement is any good.  We’ve kept the approach loose. Tool integrations to Xero, Square, and the platform’s existing systems are the co-founder’s job. The student’s job is the brain: the reasoning layer that decides what’s worth raising with managers. Modern LLM agents do well when they have the right tools and context to work with, and 14 weeks goes fast. We want the student’s time on intelligence, not on integration code.  Type: applied AI research and prototyping. The student works largely independently. Weekly check-ins and ad-hoc Slack support from the technical co-founder; methodology and dissertation support from the University-assigned academic supervisor. From day one they get read access to a dedicated research schema in the production database. The data is live and from real venues, not a teaching set. Business areas: hospitality operations, agentic AI, operational analytics.  Stack: TypeScript monorepo (Next.js and NestJS) on PostgreSQL with pgvector (NeonDB). Anthropic Claude and Voyage AI embeddings sit behind the existing document-intelligence pipeline. The technical co-founder owns the integrations to Xero, Square, and other operational systems. The student can prototype the brain in Python or TypeScript, whichever they prefer, against the read-only research schema and a documented set of tool interfaces. |
| Student deliverables | By the end of the 14 weeks:  1. The proactive brain. A working prototype of an LLM-based agent that learns each venue’s rhythm from live data and surfaces prompts managers when reality drifts. With documented methodology for the rhythm-learning component and the agent’s reasoning design.  2. An evaluation framework, with results. Methodology and findings on how the agent’s prompts performed against real signals: quantitative measures (precision, recall, calibration) plus qualitative manager feedback.  3. A technical report or dissertation: methodology, model selection, agent design, evaluation, and recommendations for what comes next. |
